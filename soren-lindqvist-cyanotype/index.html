02 // PHOTOCHEMICAL PROTOCOL

SIR JOHN HERSCHEL'S 1842 ACTINIC PROCESS • ZERO SILVER HALIDES
PHASE I // SENSITIZING CHARTREUSE EMULSION

Ferric Salt Coating

Equal volumes of 20% Ferric Ammonium Citrate (green crystal) and 8% Potassium Ferricyanide are mixed under tungsten safelight and hand-brushed onto 100% cotton rag paper. Dried in the dark, the sheet holds a luminous chartreuse-amber skin.

SENSITIZER PEAK 365–405 NM UV-A
PHASE II // CONTACT EXPOSURE NORTH SEA DAYLIGHT

Actinic Reduction

Pressed flat inside a heavy ash-wood contact frame beneath 6mm low-iron glass, botanical specimens or architectural CAD vellums block UV photons. Where light strikes the paper, iron(III) reduces to iron(II), forming insoluble Prussian blue pigment.

BERGEN OVERCAST 14–35 MIN EXPOSURE
PHASE III // CLEARING & OXIDATION PERMANENCE

Archival Acid Wash

Unexposed ferric salts wash out in cold running Bergen water, revealing stark rag-paper whites beneath the specimen shadows. Immersion in a mild citric acid bath deepens the ferric ferrocyanide lattice for century-long museum permanence.
