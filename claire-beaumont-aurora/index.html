NOV 1423:40−19°CKp 5The forecast lied beautifully+

A G1 storm, the kind that usually means a faint green smudge. Instead the whole sky moved for two hours. Two lessons about spare batteries: keep them inside your jacket, not your bag, and bring a second set for the second set.

OCT 3001:15−11°CKp 2On waiting+

Four hours on the ridge and nothing. I've stopped calling those nights wasted. Four hours of nothing teaches you the shape of the dark, so when four minutes of everything arrives, you already know where to point.

SEP 1922:05+3°CKp 3A beginner's aurora, explained to my niece+

The sky is a curtain, the sun is wind, and the colours are the air glowing because it got bumped. None of it is quite right. All of it is true enough to make her want to stay up.

About Claire

I photograph the part of the night most people sleep through.

Claire Beaumont grew up in Winnipeg, where the sky is big and the winters are honest, and moved to Jasper in 2016 for a planetarium job that ended, as good jobs sometimes do, by teaching her she wanted to be outside the dome, under the real thing. She shoots on a ridge she can reach in forty minutes and a river bend she can reach in ten, and she still gets the forecast wrong about as often as the forecast does. Her work hangs in the Jasper library and, she is told, in at least one Norwegian cabin she will never see.
